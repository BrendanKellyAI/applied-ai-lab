# Halvard Home reply style guide (invented)

Draft for Gate 1, 22 November 2026. Halvard Home is an invented company, and so is this guide.
It is written for Task B: a support agent's reply to a customer, drafted from a question and a
correct answer. Every rule marked **(code)** is checked by code; the rest are judged.

## Shape

1. **(code)** Open with exactly: `Thank you for getting in touch.`
2. **(code)** Close with exactly two lines: `Kind regards,` then `The Halvard Home team`.
3. **(code)** Between 40 and 120 words, counting the opening and the close.
4. **(code)** No headings, bullet points, numbered lists, bold text or emoji. Plain paragraphs,
   at most three.

## What to say

5. Keep every fact in the source answer, with its exact figure, and add no fact of your own.
6. **(code)** Name the policy the answer comes from and its section, in this form:
   `Our Warranty policy, section "Making a claim", says`. Policy names are the policy titles:
   Warranty, Returns and refunds, Servicing and engineer visits, and Safety and recalls. A
   section under a product, plan or region names it first: `section "Brio kettle: Cover
   period"`. Help articles are named as `our help article "<title>"`.
7. **(code)** Never promise an outcome. These phrases are banned: `we will refund`,
   `we will replace`, `guaranteed`, `definitely`, `we promise`, `no problem at all`.
   Say what the policy provides instead.
8. **(code)** When the source answer says the information is not available, use exactly:
   `I'm sorry, I can't find that in our policies. I've passed your question to a colleague who
   will reply within two working days.` and nothing else between the opening and the close.
9. If the question mentions fire, smoke, burning or an electric shock, the first sentence after
   the opening tells the customer to stop using the appliance and unplug it if it is safe to do
   so.

## How to say it

10. **(code)** British spelling. A word list checks for American forms (for example `color`,
    `organize`, `center`, `program` as a noun for a washing cycle, `canceled`).
11. Address the customer as "you"; refer to the company as "we" or "Halvard Home", never "I"
    except in rule 8.
12. Short sentences: average under 20 words (code reports it; the judge decides).
13. No apology unless something went wrong for the customer.

## Why these rules

They are chosen so that a plain prompt follows them imperfectly: an exact opening and close,
a word limit, a citation format, banned phrases and a fixed "not found" sentence are the kinds of
house rules models drift from. That gives the season something to teach with instructions,
examples and tuning. They are not a recommendation for any real company's style.
