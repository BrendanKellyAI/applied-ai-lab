# Goodwill and pricing rules

## Brio kettle

### Goodwill and discounts

Use goodwill to put things right when a customer has had a disappointing experience with the Brio kettle. Listen to the concern, check the order details and establish what has happened before suggesting a remedy. Be courteous and factual, and avoid promising an outcome while the circumstances are still unclear.

Staff may waive charges on the Brio kettle of up to £73.00 as goodwill. Check which charges apply and whether they can be waived through the approved process. Explain clearly what the waiver covers, and do not describe it as a refund unless money is actually being returned.

The largest discount staff may offer on the Brio kettle is 17%. Check the current selling price and any promotion attached to the order before offering a discount. Use the approved pricing tool rather than calculating or changing the price outside the sales system.

Choose a remedy that fits the issue. A goodwill offer should acknowledge the customer’s experience, not reward pressure or discourage a legitimate complaint. Apply the same principles consistently, while considering the circumstances recorded against the order.

Do not combine a goodwill waiver with a discount without approval from your manager. Check the order history for existing adjustments so that a previous concession is not duplicated. If the system cannot apply the intended remedy correctly, pause and ask for help rather than using a workaround.

Record the reason for the offer, the checks completed and any approval received. Keep notes factual and respectful. Make clear whether the customer has accepted the offer, and ensure the order record matches what you have told them.

Goodwill must not replace the customer’s statutory rights or any remedy they are entitled to under the applicable terms. Do not make access to those rights conditional on accepting a goodwill offer, withdrawing a complaint or leaving a favourable review.

Refer requests outside your authority to your manager without implying that approval is guaranteed. If a safety concern is raised about the Brio kettle, follow the safety reporting process rather than treating a pricing adjustment as a resolution.

## Tessa dishwasher

### Goodwill and discounts

Staff may waive charges on the Tessa dishwasher of up to £113.00 as goodwill. Use this discretion where a customer has experienced inconvenience, avoidable confusion or a service shortcoming. Check the facts and explain how the proposed waiver responds to the customer’s experience.

The largest discount staff may offer on the Tessa dishwasher is 42%. Treat this as a limit, not a standard offer or a starting point for negotiation. Choose a discount that is proportionate to the circumstances and supported by a clear reason.

Check the customer’s order, the current selling price and any existing offers before discussing a reduction. Make sure you understand which charges would be waived and which part of the price would be discounted. Do not promise an adjustment if its effect on the customer’s bill is unclear.

Do not split adjustments, relabel a discount as goodwill or combine concessions to get around the applicable limit. Where a goodwill waiver and a discount are being considered together, ask a manager to confirm whether the proposed arrangement is permitted before making a commitment.

Goodwill must not replace a remedy the customer is entitled to under their legal rights or applicable guarantee terms. Establish whether the issue should be handled as a fault, damage, delivery problem or complaint before deciding whether a discretionary concession is appropriate.

Explain the offer in plain language. Tell the customer what it covers, what remains payable and whether accepting it affects any optional arrangements. Do not suggest that accepting goodwill removes their legal rights. Avoid promises about approval, refunds or payment processing that you cannot support.

Record the reason for the concession, the relevant circumstances, the adjustment agreed and any approval obtained. Keep notes factual and respectful. Include enough detail for colleagues to understand the decision without asking the customer to repeat their account.

If the customer requests something outside your authority, acknowledge the request and refer it to a manager. Explain that you cannot guarantee approval. Apply the same approach fairly across customers, and never make a concession conditional on a favourable review or the withdrawal of a legitimate complaint.
