# Goodwill and pricing rules

## Brio kettle

### Goodwill and discounts

Use goodwill to put a customer’s experience right when something has gone wrong with their Brio kettle purchase or with the service they received. Listen to the concern, check the relevant order and service notes, and establish what outcome the customer is seeking before discussing a concession.

Staff may waive charges on the Brio kettle of up to £67.00 as goodwill. Check the charges actually due and explain clearly which charge you propose to waive. Do not describe a waived charge as a refund if the customer has not paid it. If payment has already been taken, check the refund process before making a commitment.

The largest discount staff may offer on the Brio kettle is 32%. Confirm the applicable selling price and check whether a promotion or an existing concession affects the proposed discount. Use the approved pricing process rather than working out an alternative arrangement outside the sales system.

Do not combine a goodwill waiver with a discount to get around these limits. Check the customer’s record for concessions already agreed on the same purchase. If the combined effect is unclear, or the requested outcome falls outside your authority, refer the case to your manager before promising anything.

Keep the reason for the decision factual and relevant. Record the customer’s concern, the checks you made, the concession agreed and any approval obtained. Avoid personal judgements about the customer, and do not include sensitive information that is unnecessary for handling the case.

Explain the offer in plain language, including how it will be applied and what the customer needs to do. Make clear whether you are proposing a waiver or a discount. Check that the customer understands the arrangement before updating the order or account.

Goodwill must not replace a remedy the customer is legally entitled to receive. Follow the relevant returns and fault handling procedures where applicable. If you are unsure whether the issue concerns a legal entitlement or a discretionary concession, seek advice rather than presenting goodwill as the customer’s only option.

## Tessa dishwasher

### Goodwill and discounts

Staff may waive charges on the Tessa dishwasher of up to £66.00 as goodwill. Use this discretion where a customer has experienced inconvenience, poor service or an avoidable problem linked to their purchase. A waiver should address the circumstances fairly, rather than serve as a routine incentive or a substitute for putting a fault right.

Check the customer’s account and the relevant purchase details before making an offer. Read the service notes, confirm what happened and establish whether goodwill has already been provided for the same issue. Ask for clarification where records are unclear. Avoid asking the customer to repeat information that is already available.

The largest discount staff may offer on the Tessa dishwasher is 35%. Treat this as a ceiling, not a standard offer. Choose a discount that is proportionate to the reason for the adjustment and explain that reason in the account notes. Do not promise an offer until you have checked that it is within your authority.

Do not combine a goodwill waiver with a discount to get around either limit. Check whether an existing promotion or agreed price adjustment affects the proposed offer. If the combined effect is unclear, pause and ask your manager to review it before discussing a final price with the customer.

Explain clearly whether you are waiving a charge or reducing the selling price. Tell the customer which charge or price the adjustment applies to, and confirm the agreed outcome in writing. Use straightforward language and avoid describing discretionary goodwill as a legal entitlement or a guaranteed response to future complaints.

Goodwill must not replace the customer’s statutory rights. Where the customer reports a fault or asks about a repair, replacement or refund, follow the relevant process separately. Do not make goodwill conditional on the customer giving up those rights, withdrawing a complaint or leaving a favourable review.

Record the reason for the offer, the adjustment applied and any approval received. Keep notes factual and respectful. If the requested outcome falls outside these rules, refer it to your manager without suggesting that approval is assured. Apply the same fair approach regardless of how firmly a customer presses their case.
