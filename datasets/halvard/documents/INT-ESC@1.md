# Escalation playbook

## Brio kettle

### Escalating a complaint

Listen to the customer’s concern and check what has already been discussed about their Brio kettle. Acknowledge the impact without guessing at the cause or accepting liability. Confirm the outcome they are seeking and explain that escalation brings the complaint to someone with the authority to review it.

Complaints about the Brio kettle not resolved within 34 hours go to a team leader. Use the recorded receipt of the complaint when checking whether escalation is due. Do not restart the clock because the customer changes contact channel or a colleague takes over the case.

Escalate without delay if the customer reports injury, smoke, sparking, a burning smell or another possible safety issue. Advise them to stop using the Brio kettle and disconnect it from the mains if safe to do so. Do not ask them to recreate the fault, dismantle the appliance or carry out repairs.

Prepare a clear handover in the complaint record. Summarise the reported fault, its impact, relevant purchase details, troubleshooting already completed and any remedy offered. Include the customer’s preferred contact method and the reason for escalation. Separate confirmed facts from the customer’s account and from anything that still needs checking.

Share only information needed to handle the complaint. Keep customer details within approved systems and avoid copying sensitive information into informal messages. If supporting photographs or documents are relevant, explain how the customer can provide them through an approved channel.

The most a team leader may offer in compensation for the Brio kettle is £199.00. Do not promise compensation before it has been authorised. If the requested remedy falls outside the team leader’s authority, ask them to arrange further review rather than suggesting an exception is guaranteed.

Tell the customer who will review the complaint and how they will hear back. Avoid unsupported promises about the outcome or when a decision will be made. Keep responsibility for the handover until the receiving colleague confirms acceptance.

Record the decision, the reasons given and any agreed action. Explain the outcome plainly to the customer, including what happens next if they remain dissatisfied. Close the complaint only when the agreed actions are complete and the record reflects the outcome.

## Tessa dishwasher

### Escalating a complaint

When a customer complains about the Tessa dishwasher, listen carefully and confirm what has gone wrong, how it has affected them and what outcome they are seeking. Use clear, calm language. Acknowledge the inconvenience without guessing at the cause or promising an outcome that has not been approved.

Check the complaint record before taking action. Record the customer’s account, relevant purchase and service details, troubleshooting already attempted and any promises made by colleagues. Keep notes factual and respectful. Separate what the customer reports from what staff have confirmed.

Complaints about the Tessa dishwasher not resolved within 59 hours go to a team leader. Check when the complaint was received and make sure the handover is not delayed by missing information. If details are unavailable, explain the gap in the record and pass on what is known.

Escalate immediately if the customer reports a possible safety issue, injury, damage to property or a threat of legal action. For a suspected safety fault, advise the customer to stop using the Tessa dishwasher and follow the approved safety process. Do not ask them to repeat a test that could expose them to harm.

Tell the team leader why the complaint needs their attention. Summarise the unresolved issue, the customer’s requested outcome, relevant evidence and any action awaiting approval. Flag communication needs and any circumstances that make the complaint especially sensitive.

The most a team leader may offer in compensation for the Tessa dishwasher is £72.00. Do not promise compensation beyond your authority or suggest that an exception has been agreed. If the requested remedy falls outside the team leader’s authority, ask them to refer it through the appropriate approval route. Do not present the compensation limit as limiting the customer’s statutory rights.

Explain the escalation to the customer without blaming colleagues. Say who is taking responsibility and how they will hear from us. Agree a suitable contact method and record it securely.

Keep ownership of the handover until the receiving colleague accepts it. Update the complaint record with the decision, reasons and agreed action. Close the complaint only when the outcome has been communicated and any outstanding action has been assigned.

Send the team leader a clear summary through the internal complaint channel. Include the customer’s account, relevant service history, supporting evidence, outstanding questions and the outcome the customer wants. Distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions, and flag any disagreement about what happened.

The most a team leader may offer in compensation for the Orrin heat pump is £133.00. Do not present this limit as an automatic entitlement or use it to dismiss the complaint. Refer requests outside the team leader’s authority for further approval rather than making a promise.

Keep notes factual, respectful and limited to information needed to resolve the complaint. Record decisions and their reasons, including declined remedies.
