# Known-faults register

## Brio kettle

### Faults under investigation

The current fault rate on returned Brio kettle units is 10%. This figure relates to returned units and should not be presented as the fault rate across Brio kettles in use. Do not use it to estimate a customer’s likelihood of experiencing a fault.

The investigation remains open. Record the customer’s description in their own words, then separate reported symptoms from findings confirmed through inspection. Avoid treating similar descriptions as proof that the underlying cause is the same.

Ask what happened during normal use, whether the behaviour can be described clearly, and whether there are signs of leakage, overheating, damage or unexpected operation. Do not ask a customer to recreate a fault or operate a kettle that may be unsafe.

Where safety is in doubt, advise the customer to stop using the Brio kettle and disconnect it from the mains if safe to do so. Escalate the case through the product safety process. Do not suggest opening the kettle, bypassing protective features or attempting a home repair.

For returned units, attach the case reference and preserve the reported condition. Follow approved handling and inspection procedures. Keep potentially unsafe returns out of demonstration stock and prevent their return to sale while assessment is pending.

Record photographs, inspection findings and relevant customer correspondence in the approved case system. Keep personal information out of informal messages. Make clear whether an observation came from the customer, a colleague or an authorised assessment.

A fix for the open fault on the Brio kettle is expected within 156 days. Treat this as an investigation forecast, not a guaranteed resolution date or a promise that a particular customer’s kettle can be repaired.

Use approved customer wording when discussing the investigation. Explain what action is available under the applicable returns or warranty process without implying that the suspected fault has been confirmed. Refer requests for technical conclusions to the investigation team.

Check the register before giving advice, and flag contradictions between the recorded guidance and the evidence in a case. Submit new findings through the agreed escalation route rather than circulating an unofficial diagnosis or workaround.
